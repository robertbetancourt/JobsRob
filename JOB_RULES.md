# JOB_RULES.md

## Purpose

Define the decision framework used by Job Radar to discover, filter, score, and prioritize job opportunities for Robert Betancourt.

The system is a decision-support tool, not an automatic application system. AI may evaluate and explain opportunities, but Robert makes the final decision.

## 1. Core Objective

Find paid work opportunities that offer a reasonable combination of:
- Compensation
- Sustainable working conditions
- Role fit
- Location/work authorization compatibility
- Reasonable scope
- Stability
- Growth or experience

The goal is not to maximize salary alone or maximize the number of applications. The goal is to maximize the quality of opportunities presented to Robert.

Approximately 5 years in Product Design / UI/UX.

Core capabilities:
- Product Design
- UX/UI
- User flows
- Information architecture
- High-fidelity UI
- Prototyping
- Design systems
- Responsive design
- Mobile products

- Product thinking
- Translating business requirements into user experiences
- Collaboration with developers and product teams

Strong domain experience:
- Fintech

- B2B
- B2C

- Digital products
- Mobile applications
- Websites

These domains are strong positive signals, not hard requirements.

## 3. Target Roles

Primary roles:
- Product Designer
- Senior Product Designer
- UX Designer
- UI/UX Designer
- UX/UI Product Designer
- Digital Product Designer
- Similar product-focused design roles

The system may include Junior or Mid-level roles when the overall opportunity is strong.

Do not reject a role solely because its seniority is below Robert's experience if compensation, scope, conditions, and growth are reasonable.

Do not assume a Senior title automatically makes an opportunity better.

## 4. Employment Type

Potentially acceptable:
- Full-time
- Contract
- Part-time
- Temporary
- Freelance/project-based

Employment type should be evaluated together with compensation, stability, workload, and scope.

Unpaid work is not acceptable.

## 5. Compensation

### Important principle

There is NO universal salary hard floor.

Robert's preferred target may be around $2,000–$2,500+ USD/month for strong international opportunities, but this is an expectation, not an absolute minimum.

A lower salary can still be acceptable when overall conditions are reasonable, especially for local/LATAM opportunities.

Evaluate compensation relative to:
- Country/location
- Expected working hours
- Workload
- Responsibilities
- Seniority
- Benefits
- Stability
- Employment type
- Required availability

Examples:
- $600/month + Venezuela + approximately 8h/day + Monday–Friday + reasonable Product Design scope: potentially acceptable.
- $600/month + 12–14h/day + weekends + constant availability + design/development/marketing responsibilities: strong negative / likely reject.
- $2,500/month + reasonable 8h/day + Product Design scope: strong positive.
- $3,000/month + extreme workload and constant availability: do not assume it is better than the $2,500 opportunity.

If salary is undisclosed, do NOT automatically reject.

## 6. Working Hours & Boundaries

Preferred baseline:
- Approximately 8 hours/day
- Approximately 40 hours/week for full-time roles
- Clear working expectations
- Respect for personal time
- No expectation of unpaid work outside normal hours
- No routine unpaid weekend work

Strong negative signals:
- 12–14+ hour days
- Mandatory weekend work without appropriate compensation
- Constant availability
- Frequent late-night requests
- 24/7/on-call expectations for a normal design role
- "Must always be available"
- Unclear or intentionally excessive working expectations

Weekend or after-hours work is not automatically unacceptable if explicitly occasional, compensated, and appropriate to the role.

## 7. Location & Work Arrangement

Robert is based in Venezuela.

Preferred:
- Remote
- International remote
- LATAM-friendly remote
- Companies explicitly open to international contractors

Hybrid:
- Hybrid in Venezuela: acceptable.
- Hybrid in another country: only realistically viable if relocation support, visa support, or another practical relocation path exists.
- Relocation may be attractive if compensation and conditions justify it.

On-site roles are not automatically rejected, but require a realistic relocation path.

If a role requires authorization/residency in a country where Robert does not have it, treat this as a major constraint.

Do not confuse working remotely from Venezuela for an international company with being legally authorized to work physically in the United States, Canada, EU, etc.

## 8. Industry

No industry is mandatory.

Remain open to opportunities across industries.

Strong positive relevance:
- Fintech
- Banking
- SaaS
- B2B
- B2C
- Marketplaces
- Mobile
- Web products

An unfamiliar industry should not be penalized heavily if the Product Design problem is strong.

## 9. Company

Company type is flexible:
- Startup
- Scale-up
- Enterprise
- Agency
- Consultancy
- Small business
- Unknown company
- Established company

Company prestige has LOW importance.

Do not prioritize a famous company over an unknown company merely because of brand recognition.

An unknown company offering strong compensation, reasonable conditions, and meaningful product work may be preferable to a prestigious company offering poor conditions.

## 10. Design Team

Team structure is neutral.

Robert can work effectively:
- As the only designer
- With one other designer
- Within a larger design team
- Cross-functionally with product and engineering

Do not penalize a role because it lacks a large design organization.

## 11. Role Scope

Positive signals:
- Product Design ownership
- UX + UI
- End-to-end design
- Product thinking
- User flows
- Complex workflows
- Design systems
- Prototyping
- Collaboration with product/engineering
- Clear product responsibilities

Negative signals:
- Excessive unrelated responsibilities
- Design + full-stack development + branding + marketing + social media + video without appropriate compensation
- "Do everything" expectations
- Product Designer role that is primarily graphic/marketing production
- Significant engineering responsibility disguised as design work

Multidisciplinary work is not automatically negative. Evaluate scope against compensation and working conditions.

## 12. Work Types to Treat Carefully

Potentially low-priority or negative:
- Graphic-design-heavy roles
- Social media design
- Banner production
- Branding-only roles
- Marketing production-only roles
- UX research-only roles
- UI production with no product/UX responsibility
- Roles with extremely broad "wear every hat" expectations

These are not automatic rejects unless the role is fundamentally incompatible with Product Design.

## 13. Application Effort

Application effort matters because Robert has limited time and should not spend hours on low-probability opportunities.

Low effort:
- Resume + portfolio
- Resume + a few questions
- Short application
Positive signal.

Medium effort:
- Resume
- Portfolio
- Cover letter
- Several questions
Neutral.

High effort:
- 1–2 hour take-home assignment
- Extensive questionnaire
- Video interview/application
Negative unless opportunity quality justifies it.

Very high effort:
- 3–4+ hour design challenge
- Multiple take-home stages
- Large unpaid deliverables
Only consider when the opportunity is genuinely strong.

A legitimate interview take-home assignment is NOT automatically the same as unpaid work.

Reject clearly unpaid production work, such as designing real screens/assets for an upcoming product.

## 14. Candidate Volume

High applicant counts are a negative signal when they substantially reduce the expected value of a time-intensive application.

Example: a 4-hour challenge for a highly competitive role with thousands of applicants should receive additional scrutiny.

Applicant count alone should NOT automatically reject a role.

Consider:
- Applicant count
- Company quality
- Role fit
- Compensation
- Application effort
- Evidence that applications are actually reviewed

## 15. Portfolio Value

Portfolio value is useful but NOT a primary decision factor.

Robert can continue improving his portfolio independently.

A job does not need to produce portfolio material to be valuable.

## 16. Personal Goals

Possible positive outcomes include:
- Good compensation
- Stability
- Professional growth
- Learning
- Strong experience
- Interesting product problems
- Good working environment
- Better long-term opportunities
- Reasonable work/life boundaries

No single outcome is mandatory.

## 17. Red Flags

Strong warning signals:

Compensation / work:
- Unpaid work
- Unpaid internship
- Equity-only compensation
- Commission-only compensation for a design role
- Very low compensation relative to workload
- Extreme hours
- Mandatory unpaid weekends
- Constant availability

Hiring process:
- Unpaid production work disguised as a test
- Excessively long take-home assignments
- Suspicious recruiter behavior
- Requests for inappropriate personal/financial information
- Unclear company identity
- No credible company presence
- Contradictory job information
- Pressure to move immediately to suspicious communication channels

Job scope:
- Designer + developer + marketer + brand designer + social media manager for low compensation
- Extremely broad responsibilities with no corresponding compensation
- Role title does not match actual work

A red flag is not always an automatic reject. Distinguish severe red flags from mild concerns.

## 18. Positive Signals

- Explicitly remote
- Explicitly accepts LATAM
- International contractor support
- Clear compensation
- Reasonable working hours
- Respect for personal time
- Product Design ownership
- Strong UX + UI responsibilities
- Complex product problems
- Clear responsibilities
- Reasonable application process
- Good compensation relative to workload
- Flexible schedule
- Relocation support for international roles
- Transparent hiring process

## 19. Decision Framework

### Layer 1 — Hard Filters

Automatically remove or heavily flag:
- Clearly unpaid work
- Clearly fraudulent/suspicious opportunities
- Impossible work authorization requirements
- Clearly extreme/unreasonable working conditions
- Compensation clearly incompatible with stated workload
- Roles fundamentally unrelated to Product/UX/UI Design

### Layer 2 — AI Evaluation

Evaluate:
- Role fit
- Compensation quality
- Work conditions
- Location compatibility
- Experience/seniority fit
- Scope quality
- Application effort
- Company quality
- Potential warnings or missing information

### Layer 3 — Robert Review

Robert makes the final decision.

The AI must NEVER automatically submit an application.

## 20. AI Score

The AI Score represents overall opportunity quality, not simply salary or keyword matching.

Initial scoring dimensions:
- Role fit
- Compensation & conditions
- Location compatibility
- Experience/seniority fit
- Scope quality
- Application effort
- Company quality
- Missing information or evidence constraints

Weights must strictly follow the canonical 100-point model.

The 100-point canonical weighting system is final.

## 21. AI Output

For every shortlisted opportunity, the system should be capable of explaining:

### Score
Example: 87 / 100

### Verdict
- Apply
- Strong match
- Review
- Low priority
- Skip

### Why it matches
Concise explanation based on actual job information.

### Concerns
Specific risks or unknowns.

### Compensation assessment
Explain compensation relative to workload and location when data is available.

### Location assessment
Explain whether the role appears compatible with Robert's location/work arrangement.

### Application effort
Estimate effort based on known application requirements.

### Recommended portfolio projects
Where possible, identify relevant projects such as:
- B89
- Banexcoin
- GIP
- Autoandes
- BE FIT
- Mimik

Only recommend projects when there is a reasonable connection to the job.

## 22. Unknown Information

Missing information should NOT automatically be treated as negative.

Examples:
- Salary undisclosed
- Applicant count unavailable
- Team size unknown
- Benefits unknown
- Exact schedule unknown

Label these as:
> Unknown

Surface them for human review when they materially affect the decision.

Never invent missing job information.

## 23. Human Feedback

Robert's decisions should be stored.

Possible decisions:
- Apply
- Skip
- Save
- Review later

Possible reasons:
- Compensation
- Location
- Work authorization
- Working hours
- Scope
- Seniority
- Application effort
- Company
- Suspicious
- Not interesting
- Other

This feedback can later be used to improve scoring and ranking.

## 24. Core Principle

Job Radar should optimize for:

> Good opportunity × reasonable conditions × realistic eligibility × worthwhile effort

Not:
> Maximum salary

and not:
> Maximum number of applications.

The system exists to protect Robert's time and help him find opportunities that are actually worth pursuing.

## 25. Product Constraint

Job Radar is a personal decision-support tool.

It should:
- Discover opportunities
- Normalize job data
- Remove duplicates
- Apply hard filters
- Score opportunities
- Explain recommendations
- Track Robert's decisions

It should NOT:
- Automatically apply to jobs
- Fabricate application answers
- Misrepresent Robert's experience
- Circumvent application restrictions
- Treat AI scoring as final truth
